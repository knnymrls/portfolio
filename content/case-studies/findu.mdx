<CaseStudySection title="Overview">

Off the title you might be wondering, well what is FindU? FindU—or as we jokingly describe it, "Tinder for Colleges"—started off as a classroom project and turned into a startup run by myself and my cofounder Wilson Overfield.

It solves the issue of students not knowing what to do after high school, and is currently focused on helping students get into college. We've raised money for FindU and built a team of more than 15 people, and learned so so much along the way!

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection title="Problem">

After graduating high school, there really is this next choice of "what do I want to do with my life". Some students know exactly what they want to do, but others don't. In fact, our main passion towards FindU came from us knowing friends and family struggling with this decision. We started off FindU trying to figure out what were the main pain points which led us to interview 50 students from all different groups.

### Customer Interviews

We spent weeks talking to students—honor roll kids, athletes, first-gen college students, kids who had no idea what they wanted to do. We did one-on-ones, group sessions, and even just casual conversations. Our conversations were increadibly open-ended and we really just let the students talk about their experiences.

What surprised us was how similar everyone's struggles were, regardless of background. The straight-A student and the kid barely passing were both equally lost when it came to figuring out what's next.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection>

### What We Heard

The same things kept coming up in our discussions:

- "I don't know how financial aid works"
- "How do I get scholarships"
- "I just don't know where I want to go"
- "My parents want me to go to a 'good school' but I don't even know what that means"
- "I feel like I'm already behind"

This is particularly interesting because these were more of the *effects* of inaction by the students. Which led us to really diagnose the problem to be: **Students don't want to spend time researching colleges.**

Now this really stems from a variety of reasons like a fear of failure, feeling overwhelmed, and simply just not knowing what is out there. In short, the information is out there, it's just not packaged in a way that would make it more accessible to students.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection title="Solution">

### Swiping

Here is where the "Tinder for Colleges" comes in. The main goal was to make a product that was engaging and students could relate to. Now traditionally students would use tools like Niche and US News which ultimately gives schools in a list and ranks them. So when students are looking for colleges they usually end up looking at the top 100 universities in the US, in a basic list.

We realized that the top 100 universities might not be a great option for students so we really wanted to hone in on **personalization**. Which is where the Tinder aspect came in—students would make a profile and then swipe through schools that fit *them* rather than the arbitrary rankings.

#### Design Evolution

Now to really nail down the design I went through countless iterations to try to find the perfect way of presenting schools.

**Version 1**

On our first version the biggest limitation really came from my lack of design skills. You'll see things like different icon sizes, varying icon packs, and overall just a bad use of space. From a UX perspective, it's overwhelming and offers no improvement other than the Tinder swiping.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection>

**Version 3**

Version 3 saw a pretty big jump in my design skills and the UX of the product. From a development standpoint this was also our beta launch where we received a lot of important feedback.

At this point, we really didn't receive any feedback on the design, but rather on the UX of the app. One thing we noticed was that most people were just swiping and not actually viewing the "more info" button. Additionally, the match score also was unnecessary as students expected good schools on their feed anyway.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection>

**Version 7 (Current)**

The current version introduced a lot of changes. The first one was removing the swipeable card and instead just displaying the information. From a UX perspective, this reduces a lot of friction and makes it more about the information than simply swiping.

We also:
- Broke down the information into sections
- Allowed users to filter by location, costs, etc.
- Introduced a saved page so students could track their schools
- Added question cards in the feed to improve matches and recommendations

I really want to emphasize these improvements have been over the course of the last year, and have been fueled by hundreds of interviews from students. And by no means is this the final version!

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection>

### Scholarships

We built a comprehensive scholarship matching system that connects students with opportunities they actually qualify for, not just a generic list.

### Messaging

Direct communication between students and admissions counselors, making the process feel more personal and less intimidating.

### AI Assistant

An AI-powered assistant that answers questions about colleges, applications, and the entire process in a conversational way.

### Tasks

A task management system that keeps students on track with deadlines, requirements, and next steps.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection title="Development">

One of the biggest challenges was aggregating college data from multiple sources and keeping it accurate and up-to-date.

### Student Data

The heart of FindU—our algorithm that matches students with schools based on dozens of factors, constantly improving based on user feedback.
